Portuguese-Style Caldo Verde with Collards and Smoked Kielbasa

6

Ingredient amounts are estimates when scaled. Original recipe and AnyList import remain unchanged.

Ingredients

  • 1 lb smoked kielbasa, sliced (or Portuguese chouriço/linguiça)
  • 2 lb Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled and diced
  • 1 large yellow onion, diced
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 Tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
  • 6 cups chicken broth or water
  • 1 lb collard greens, stems removed, leaves very finely shredded
  • 1–1½ tsp sweet paprika; ¼ tsp smoked paprika (optional); salt and black pepper
  • 1–2 tsp red wine vinegar, to taste; crusty bread, to serve

Instructions

  1. INSTANT POT: Brown sliced kielbasa in olive oil; remove and reserve. Sauté onion in the flavored oil 4 minutes, then garlic 30 seconds. Stir in sweet paprika and optional smoked paprika for 20–30 seconds without burning.
  2. Add potatoes and broth, scrape bottom, and pressure-cook HIGH 8 minutes. Natural release 10 minutes, then release remaining pressure.
  3. Blend or mash potato broth until mostly smooth. Stir in finely shredded collards and sausage. Simmer on sauté 10–20 minutes or until greens are tender. Season, stir in 1 tsp red wine vinegar, taste, and add up to 1 tsp more if needed. Finish with olive oil.
  4. SLOW COOKER: Brown kielbasa in olive oil and reserve. Sauté onion and garlic, then briefly bloom the paprika off direct high heat before adding broth. Cook potatoes with broth LOW 6–7 hours or HIGH 3–4 hours. Blend or mash potatoes, add greens and reserved kielbasa for last 45–90 minutes on HIGH until tender. Finish with red wine vinegar to taste.
  5. Serve with crusty bread. For soccer transport keep hot at 140°F or higher, or chill and reheat to 165°F.

Notes

Traditional caldo verde uses couve galega and chouriço. This version uses collards and smoked kielbasa. Sweet paprika restores some of the chouriço-style seasoning; use smoked paprika sparingly because kielbasa is already smoked. Add red wine vinegar at the end, starting with 1 tsp, to brighten the soup. If using chouriço instead, omit the added paprika and vinegar unless needed to taste. Potatoes thicken the soup, not cream.
