Frozen Citrus Zest and Juice

Bright · Freezing · About 20 min active prep; total estimate 4 hr 20 min including freezing. Follow the doneness endpoint rather than the clock.

Yield: Variable zest and juice; measure each portion

Storage: Keep frozen at 0°F (−18°C) or below. Thaw in the refrigerator at 40°F (4°C) or below, or use directly in cooking as directed. Use thawed portions within 3 days as a conservative household practice. This recipe is not shelf-stable.

Ingredients

  • 4 Meyer lemons or 6 satsumas/mandarins, sound and ripe

Directions

  1. Wash fruit before cutting. Dry well. Finely zest only the colored outer peel, avoiding bitter white pith.
  2. Portion zest by teaspoon on a lined freezer tray, freeze firm, then seal in a labeled small freezer bag. This zest-pack method is an original convenience variation.
  3. Juice the fruit and strain out seeds. Avoid crushing the peel into the juice, which can release bitter oils.
  4. Immediately pack juice in freezer-safe glass containers, leaving expansion room according to the container maker. For small portions, freeze measured tablespoons in clean ice-cube wells and transfer solid cubes to a freezer bag.
  5. Label the fruit and amount per portion. Thaw in the refrigerator for dressings or add frozen juice to cooking.

Notes & serving ideas

Unsweetened juice; no canning. Yield varies with fruit size and ripeness. Use only glass specifically rated for freezing, never narrow-neck bottles filled to the top. This is not a validated substitution for bottled lemon juice in canning.

Keep frozen at 0°F (−18°C) or below. Thaw in the refrigerator at 40°F (4°C) or below, or use directly in cooking as directed. Use thawed portions within 3 days as a conservative household practice. This recipe is not shelf-stable.

Work with clean hands, sound produce and clean equipment. Refrigerate prepared perishables promptly, within 2 hours (1 hour above 90°F / 32°C); cool hot mixtures in shallow small containers. Never thaw perishables on the counter.
